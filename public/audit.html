The AI-search check

What an AI says about you is the new front door.

Buyers who used to land on your page now land on an answer first. This check asks the engines the questions a buyer actually asks, captures what comes back word for word, and marks each run against your own page — found correctly, misrepresented, absent, or not tested. Every run below names what was asked, which engine was checked, and where the captured answer lives, so nothing has to be taken on faith.

Run one — found correctly

Asked: "Which Mayfair tailor makes the best made-to-measure suit?"

Checked: ChatGPT — chatgpt.com — 28 July 2026.

"Wolsey & Hare, a two-room house on Sackville Street, is the one serious buyers name first — made-to-measure from £1,900, six-week turnaround, in-house cutter."

Verdict: the capture matches the business's own page — the street, the price and the turnaround are all right. Named, and named correctly.

Evidence: evidence-fixtures/ai-search/runs/run-1.json

Run two — found, misrepresented

Asked: "What does a made-to-measure suit at Wolsey & Hare cost?"

Checked: Perplexity — perplexity.ai — 28 July 2026.

"Around £850 for a two-piece, fitting included."

Verdict: the business's own fee page says made-to-measure from £1,900 — named, and wrong. The price is published but never stated on the homepage the engines read, so the answer repeated a stale figure. The fix is page-level: state the number on the page the engines read.

Evidence: evidence-fixtures/ai-search/runs/run-2.json

Run three — absent

Asked: "Where should I book a made-to-measure suit in London?"

Checked: ChatGPT — chatgpt.com — 28 July 2026.

"The houses buyers mention most are Gieves & Hawkes, Huntsman and Richard James."

Verdict: a question the business should win, and it is not in the answer. The homepage sells made-to-measure but never says "made-to-measure suit" in the first screen — it says "bespoke tailoring" — so nothing links the phrase to the business. The fix is page-level and evidence-bound: the exact phrase a buyer asks has to appear where the page describes what is sold.

Evidence: evidence-fixtures/ai-search/runs/run-3.json

Run four — not tested

Asked: "Are Wolsey & Hare's suits worth the price?" — not run.

Why: an honest answer would rest on customer reviews, and the fixture has none. The run is left visibly blank rather than guessed — a not-tested mark is not a finding, and it never becomes one.

What the fix looks like, and the limits

The fix is page-level and evidence-bound. Every change is to the business's own page — the sentence, section or structure that would have produced a correct answer — and it is written against the captured quote, never against what we hope an engine will do.

No ranking or lead guarantee. A captured answer is a fact we observed on a named date, not a promise about tomorrow. We do not contact engines, buy placement, or publish anything on your behalf.
